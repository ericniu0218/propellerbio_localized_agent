Compared to previous seasons, the clinical outcomes were more favorable, as only one unvaccinated neonate with RSV infection required invasive ventilation [1]. ## Disease biology

9.4% of the infants in the nirsevimab group had at least one serious AE, compared to 10.3% in the control group [3]. All infants acquired RSV infection after discharge from the hospital [1]. The mother's age, lower parity, and having a partner in work were associated with nirsevimab acceptance [3]. The most common reason for accepting treatment was 'to protect my baby' [3]. Three consecutive RSV seasons were included in the study: 2024–2025, 2023–2024, and 2022–2023 [1]. The RSV outbreak season was defined as November to April [1]. A total of 2035 consecutive newborns were enrolled in the study [1]. The collected data included demographic information [1]. The exclusion criteria included missing or incomplete medical records [1]. The inclusion criteria were neonates born or admitted during the study periods [1].

## Human clinical evidence

RSV-associated medically attended lower respiratory infection occurred in 60 of 2398 infants (incidence rate over 5-month period, 2.6%) vs 74 of 1201 infants (incidence rate over 5-month period, 6.5%) [5]. one unvaccinated neonate with RSV infection required invasive ventilation [1]. boosted pre-existing RSV A and RSV B immunity 12- and 14-fold [6]. RSVPreF3-specific CD4+ T-cell frequencies were increased at 1 month postvaccination [7]. RSV-A/RSV-B neutralizing titers were increased at 1 month postvaccination [7]. RSV-A/RSV-B geometric mean titer ratios were noninferior in the at-risk 18-49 vs ≥60 group at 1 month postvaccination [7]. Rates and intensity of unsolicited adverse events (AEs) were comparable across groups [7]. Solicited events were more frequent in the at-risk 18-49 group (84.3%) than in the ≥60 group (69.4%) [7]. 79 (66.9%) received either the infant or maternal immunization [4].
